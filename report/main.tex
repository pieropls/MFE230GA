\section{Executive summary}\label{sec:summary}
% One paragraph, <= 230 words: strategy, Do not implement with justification, what survives, forward test, Claude.
We propose a market-neutral industry-rotation strategy: rank 13 U.S.\ industry groups each month on the year-over-year change in openings, hires, layoffs and hours, as known at the time, and hold the top three against the bottom three. It was preregistered (Study~1) and tested once on 142 months, November 2014 to August 2026. It lost 4.6\% a year after costs (Sharpe $-0.58$); its forecasts were uncorrelated with returns and it failed four of five preset criteria. % src: claims C01, C02, C03, C07
We conclude: \emph{Do not implement}. Afterwards we found two of its four signs opposite to the data in both periods, its one significant signal, wage growth, dropped, and its AI-proposed features switched off in almost every test month. % src: claims D03, D01, D12
In a post-hoc iteration (Study~2) the data are consistent with a slower, opposite effect that investment-based asset pricing predicts: industries that hire and lose workers fastest earn lower returns over the next 9 to 24 months. A 12-month book earns a test Sharpe of $+0.29$ ($+0.20$ on data as known at the time), % src: claims V_V2d_SR, T02
but it was designed after the test was opened, lost money in the first test half and does not survive a correction for the looks taken. % src: claims R4a, T07
It runs as a paper forward test frozen 3 October 2026. % src: claim F05
Claude was a useful auditor and idea generator when a human checked every claim and it could not change the rules after seeing results. % src: the team's written Claude evaluation (Al Yazid Bensaid, 3 Oct 2026), bottom line; Appendix E

\section{What did we try? Labor flows as industry news}

\subsection{Ideas: demand news, cost news and tightness}\label{sec:ideas}

\paragraph{Origin} The idea is a macro driver turned into a cross-sectional signal. The labor market reports an industry's prospects before its earnings do: the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) publishes job openings, hires, quits and layoffs by industry every month in the Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey (JOLTS), and hours and earnings in the Current Employment Statistics (CES). We turn each series into an industry score and judge it by its information coefficient (IC), the rank correlation between the score and the next relative return. The benchmark it has to beat is industry momentum, the signal behind most published industry rotation \citep{MoskowitzGrinblatt1999,ZarattiniAntonacci2024}. We state three hypotheses (\cref{fig:thesis}).

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{H1, demand channel} (preregistered and tested). Rising openings, hires and hours signal growing demand for an industry's output before it reaches reported sales, so the industry outperforms over the next one to three months \citep{HongTorousValkanov2007}.
  \item \emph{H2, cost and investment channel} (tested post hoc). Firms that hire heavily convert growth options into assets in place and pay adjustment costs, so their expected returns fall over the following 6 to 24 months \citep{BeloLinBazdresch2014}; wage pressure squeezes margins, and labor-market frictions make labor-intensive firms riskier \citep{KuehnSimutinWang2017,Donangelo2019}.
  \item \emph{H3, tightness moderator.} When vacancies outnumber the unemployed, hiring is expensive and H2 should dominate H1. We measure tightness by $T_t=\log(\text{job openings}_t/\text{unemployed}_t)$; $T>0$ means more vacancies than unemployed ($V/U>1$).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Two studies} Study~1 traded H1: a monthly book long the industries with rising openings, hires and hours and falling layoffs, preregistered and tested once. It is the only clean out-of-sample test in this paper. Study~2 starts from its failure. The diagnosis (\cref{sec:s1}) showed that the one-month signs were wrong or absent and that hiring and quits predict returns with the opposite sign at long horizons, which is H2. Study~2 asks whether a slow cost-channel book works, with four variants written down on 3 October 2026 (\cref{tab:models}). The test period had been opened by then, so every Study~2 number carries the post-hoc label, and the only clean evidence H2 can still earn is the forward test.

\paragraph{Why Study 1 carries the paper}\label{par:carries} (a) It is the only clean out-of-sample test we have; it was run once and we report it as it came out. (b) Its failure produced the candidate: the diagnosis in \cref{sec:s1} pointed to wage growth and to the 12-month hiring reversal, and Study~2 builds on it. (c) It is the reason Study~2 must be labelled post hoc and the reason the forward test exists. (d) It contains the agent experiment, which is the original part of the design and the basis of our evaluation of Claude (\cref{sec:claude}).

\paragraph{Four data versions} Labor data are revised after first release, so today's values leak information. Study~1 rebuilt every series as it stood on each decision date from ALFRED, the St.\ Louis Fed archive of data vintages (78 series), % src: study1_preregistered/outputs/follow_the_workers/report/report.md section 2b (78 core first-vintage dates)
and kept three alternatives: first release, fully revised, and revised with fixed publication lags. Its result barely depends on the version (\cref{tab:s1robust}). Study~2 first used revised data with fixed lags, under which the fixed rule A0 picks the same top-three groups as on as-known data in only 42\% of months; % src: claim S02
Study~2.3 therefore reran every post-hoc variant on as-known data from Study~1's saved panel, after checking that our rebuild reproduces its features exactly (\cref{tab:hq12}). % src: claim T01
The rerun also removes a flaw of the fixed-lag rule, which assumes the normal release calendar: the federal shutdown of October to November 2025 delayed releases, so two of the last 18 months of the fixed-lag Study~2 numbers use data published after the decision. % src: analysis/output/study1/data_vintage.md (Known exception)

\paragraph{Universe and effective breadth} The 49 French industries are mapped to the 13 JOLTS/CES industry groups and value-weighted within each group (\cref{app:mapping}). The groups are not 13 independent bets: the participation ratio of the correlation matrix of their relative returns, $(\sum\lambda)^2/\sum\lambda^2$ over its eigenvalues, is 9.1. % src: claim T16
By the fundamental law of active management the information ratio is about $\mathrm{IC}\times\sqrt{\mathrm{BR}}\times\mathrm{TC}$, where BR is breadth (independent bets a year) and TC the transfer coefficient (the correlation between the signal and the weights held). With 9 effective groups a monthly IC of 0.05 caps the ratio near 0.5 and a 12-month IC of 0.10 near 0.3. % src: arithmetic from claim T16 (0.05 x sqrt(9.1 x 12) = 0.52; 0.10 x sqrt(9.1) = 0.30)
Even with all 13 groups counted as independent, the law implies only $+0.17$ for the 12-month book of Study~2 (TC 0.48), against a realised $+0.29$. % src: claim R11a
The mapping is also coarse: labor data describe every establishment in an industry, returns describe listed firms, and durable manufacturing is 60\% semiconductors by market capitalisation. % src: claim S03

\paragraph{Sample} Research (signal design and model selection): May 2004 to September 2014, 125 months. Test, opened once: November 2014 to August 2026, 142 months. % src: claim S05

\subsection{Implementation: signals, models, risk, costs and AI prompts}\label{sec:impl}

\paragraph{Signals} For a labor series $x_{g,t}$ in group $g$, let $\bar x_{g,t}=\tfrac13(x_{g,t}+x_{g,t-1}+x_{g,t-2})$. With publication lag $\ell$ (2 months for JOLTS, 1 for CES),
\begin{equation}
F_{g,t}=\bar x_{g,t-\ell}-\bar x_{g,t-\ell-12}\qquad\text{(log differences for hours and earnings).}
\end{equation}
Each signal is divided by its own expanding-window standard deviation (at least 24 months), z-scored across the 13 groups and winsorised at $\pm3$, giving $z^{(k)}_{g,t}$. Year-over-year differences remove seasonality.

\paragraph{Models} \Cref{tab:models} lists every strategy. The learned ones in Study~1 are ridge regressions of next-month relative return on the signals,
\begin{equation}
\hat\beta_t=\arg\min_\beta \sum_{s<t}\sum_g\big(r^{\mathrm{rel}}_{g,s+1}-z_{g,s}^{\top}\beta\big)^2+\alpha\lVert\beta\rVert^2,
\end{equation}
re-estimated each month on an expanding window, with $\alpha$ chosen once by cross-validation with a gap between folds, inside the research period; it chose 1000, the strongest shrinkage on the grid, for every model. % src: claim C18

\paragraph{How Study 2 was run} Each stage was written to a specification file, hashed (a fingerprint that changes if the file does) and committed before it ran, then run once; a run log stores the hash of every output, and the notebook stops if a later run differs (\cref{tab:specs}, appendix). Study~2.1 fixed the four variants and a reading rule, Study~2.2 a battery of seven pre-declared conditions for HQ12, and Study~2.3 the as-known rerun, the cap and rank-weight variants and the tests of the agent experiment. Every new backtest counts as one more look in the deflated Sharpe ratio.\footnote{Each table's deflated Sharpe ratio uses the looks taken when that table was run: 112 for Study 2.1 (the four variants), 132 for Study 2.2 (the HQ12 battery) and 162 for Study 2.3 (the as-known, cap and rank-weighted runs). Later counts are larger by design.} All of them use Study~1's own backtest code, which our notebook reproduces to $10^{-16}$ before anything else runs. % src: claims S04, R13

\paragraph{Risk modeling}\label{par:risk} Each month the score $s_{m,g}$ known at the end of month $m-1$ becomes a dollar-neutral tranche, long the three highest-ranked groups and short the three lowest; the book averages the last three tranches (twelve for HQ12). The risk model is a Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage covariance of the 13 groups and the market over 60 months \citep{LedoitWolf2004}. The book is hedged to zero market beta with 60-month betas, scaled to a 10\% ex-ante volatility and capped at a gross exposure of 2 (the equations are in \cref{app:study1}). The cap, not the volatility target, was the binding constraint: thirteen correlated, hedged groups carry so little risk per unit of gross that reaching 10\% would need a median gross exposure of 2.6 for A3 and 2.4 for HQ12. % src: claim T10
\Cref{tab:overlay} shows that lifting the cap restores the volatility and leaves the Sharpe ratio where it was, so the cap explains the volatility shortfall and not the losses.

\begin{keybox}[How we kept the test honest]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Every input as it was known on the decision date; the primary result is unchanged under four data versions. % src: claim C20
  \item Signals, models, the primary strategy and five pass/fail gates (G1 timing, G2 prediction, G3 incremental alpha, G4 robustness, G5 implementability) were fixed and hashed before any test data were touched, and the test was opened once. We verified the record: the hash of the freeze file equals the one stored when the test was opened, 0.04 seconds after the freeze, and 337 of the 338 frozen files in the repository still match their hashes (the exception is the human review written afterwards). % src: claims B01, B02
  \item Every result is reported whatever its sign, with a placebo, drop-one-group checks, comparisons corrected for multiple testing (Holm) and a deflated Sharpe ratio (DSR), which discounts a Sharpe ratio for the number of looks taken \citep{BaileyLopezdePrado2014}.
  \item Anything decided after seeing test results is labelled \tagp\ and was itself written to a file and hashed before it ran; the paper book is labelled \tagf.
\end{itemize}
\end{keybox}

\paragraph{AI prompts}\label{sec:ai} We used Claude (Anthropic) for the three uses the brief names. For \emph{idea generation}, blinded agents proposed new labor features from six anonymised variables in a restricted formula grammar: two arms of three agents, independent and communicating, three seeds, 108 proposals per study, with mechanical selection and a human-audited judge (design diagram and results in \cref{app:study1}). % src: claims A01, A02
For \emph{coding support}, Claude audited the data mapping and the as-of code before any result existed. For \emph{robustness design}, it red-teamed the research results. The three prompts are below (P2 is the opening of its system prompt); outputs and our evaluation are in \cref{sec:claude}, transcripts in \cref{app:transcripts}.

\section{What did we learn? Slow cost news, not fast demand news}

\begin{keybox}[Answer to the thesis]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{H1, demand channel: rejected} \tagc. The primary H1 book (A3) lost 4.6\% a year (Sharpe $-0.58$), its IC was $-0.021$ ($t=-1.08$), the placebo $p$ (the same signal shifted in time) was 0.72 and four of five gates failed. % src: claims C01, C02, C03, C05, C07
  \item \emph{H2, cost channel: consistent with the data, not established} \tagp. The hiring score has a 12-month IC of $+0.096$ ($t=2.43$; $+0.085$ on as-known data) and HQ12 is positive in all five reporting windows, but it lost money in the first half of the test and its deflated Sharpe is 0.05. A forward test is live. % src: claims V02, T03, V_V2d_SR, R4a, R12
  \item \emph{H3, tightness: untestable as designed} \tagp. $V/U$ never exceeded 1 in the research sample, so 133 of 142 test months fell in one tightness bin. % src: claim D06
\end{itemize}
\end{keybox}

Five things went wrong, all diagnosed after the fact (figures in \cref{app:study1}). \emph{Signs:} layoffs carry a positive IC and hours a negative one, the opposite of the signs the fixed rule imposed (\cref{tab:hyp}), and wage growth, dropped during the build, is the only signal whose IC exceeds 1.5 standard errors in both periods. % src: claims D03, D01

\paragraph{P1, data and code audit} The system prompt made Claude ``a careful data and code auditor'' with no tools; the prompt in \cref{sec:ai} was followed by the manifest, the crosswalk and the as-of code (38,717 characters). % src: claim A07 (prompt length)
It returned 25 numbered findings, all tested (\path{outputs/p1_review.md}): five were claims the files did not support, among them that group 10's CES series ``silently vanishes'' (584 shared, nonmissing rows were present); one led to a metadata fix (the crosswalk now separates the prescribed match from the verified French scope); the rest were documented design choices or limitations, and none was a look-ahead error. % src: claim A07

\paragraph{P2, signal agents} Six agents (three independent, three communicating) over three seeds proposed six features each in a 14-operation grammar, blind to names and dates, with research-period feedback only. 98 of 108 proposals passed validation; the blinding audit of all 199 calls passed (108 proposals, 18 migration reports, one syntax repair). % src: claims A01, A08
The communicating agents exchanged migration reports capped at 120 words; 11 of 18 were rejected, 10 of them for running to 121--133 words although the prompt asked the agent to check its count. % src: claim A09
Selection was mechanical, two per arm from seed 1 only, and no engineer edited a proposal. Both independent-arm picks, decoded after the test, were low-tightness gates: \texttt{regime(yoy(Earnings),"low")} and \texttt{regime(spread(tsz(Layoffs,60),tsz(Hires,60)),"low")}, active in 3 of 142 test months. % src: claim D12; analysis/output/study1/tables/d7_selected_features.csv
The agents optimised what they were shown: IC split by tightness tercile. Blinded, they could not know that $T$ would stay high after 2014. Next time, keep regime splits out of agent feedback, or require a minimum share of active months in research. (We tested the first suggestion in Study~2.3; see \cref{sec:claude} and \cref{tab:r9}.)

\paragraph{P3, judge} In Study~1b, items 2, 3 and 13 cite ``submission 2's significant negative IC'', a result that exists in another report; item 12 cites ``submission 4'' from a report with two submissions. Items 8, 16 and 17 are judgment calls (a methodological tip reused; a report's topic shared but its result contradicted). The same proposal paired with two reports (items 12 and 17) got a yes both times, which is what a judge that matches on vocabulary would do. With 17 dependent pairs the sample is small. In Study~1 only 8 of 36 judge outputs were usable and 8 pairs were audited. The judge said ``uses a claim'' 10 times where the human said 3, so its 62\% uptake rate overstates communication between agents. % src: claims A01, A02, A03, A04; human_review_submission.md (Study 1b)

\section{Executive summary}
% Template: 1 paragraph. State the strategy; if no alpha, say "Do not implement" + why.
% Romain owns the final wording. Every number below must keep its % src comment.

We test whether public labor-market flows---job openings, hires, quits, layoffs, hours and
wages from the BLS JOLTS and CES surveys, measured \emph{as they were known} on each decision
date---predict which of 13 U.S.\ industry groups outperform over the next one to three months,
and whether labor-market tightness changes the answer. A preregistered, market-neutral
industry rotation was frozen on research data (2004--2014) and evaluated once on 142 untouched
months (November 2014--August 2026). It lost money: the primary strategy returned
$-4.6\%$ a year after costs (Sharpe $-0.58$), % src: follow_the_workers/outputs/sealed/initial/results.json variants.ASOF.A3
its forecasts were uncorrelated with realised returns, and a placebo test cannot tell it apart
from noise ($p=0.72$). % src: results.json placebo.p
\textbf{We conclude: Do not implement.} The failure is informative. (i)~The prespecified signs
were wrong for two of four signals, and the one labor variable with a stable link to
returns---\emph{wage growth}, rank IC $+0.04$ in both the research and the test period---had been
dropped during the build; restored post hoc, a plain wage-growth tilt earns a Sharpe of
about \prov{0.25} in both periods, % src: analysis/output/v2_book.md  (quick check 3 Oct: +0.23 test / +0.36 research)
which we freeze for a live forward test rather than claim as alpha. (ii)~Hiring behaves as
investment-based asset pricing predicts at a 12-month horizon (more hiring, lower subsequent
returns), not at the one-month horizon we traded. (iii)~Our research sample never contained a
tight labor market ($\VU>1$ in 0 research months versus 75 test months), so the tightness
hypothesis could not be tested as designed. (iv)~In a blinded experiment, LLM agents searched
for new labor signals: a more capable model followed the protocol far better but found no more
out-of-sample signal, and every agent feature that passed research failed the holdout.

\subsection{Idea: labor flows as early, public, industry-level news}

Firms reveal their plans in the labor market before they reveal them in earnings. Hiring,
quitting and layoffs are published monthly by industry, are free, and are rarely used in
industry-rotation strategies, which typically rely on price momentum
\citep{MoskowitzGrinblatt1999,ZarattiniAntonacci2024}. Two economic channels predict
\emph{opposite} signs, which is what makes the question interesting (\cref{fig:thesis}):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Demand news} (short horizon, positive sign). Rising openings, hires and hours
        signal growing demand for an industry's output before it reaches reported sales.
  \item \textbf{Cost and investment news} (longer horizon, negative sign). In
        investment-based models, firms that hire heavily are converting growth options into
        assets in place and face higher adjustment costs, so their expected returns fall
        \citep{BeloLinBazdresch2014}. Wage pressure squeezes margins. Conversely, labor-market
        frictions make labor-intensive firms riskier and raise their required returns
        \citep{KuehnSimutinWang2017,Donangelo2019}.
  \item \textbf{Tightness as moderator.} When vacancies outnumber the unemployed, hiring is
        expensive and wage news should dominate demand news. We measure tightness by
        $T_t=\log(\text{job openings}_t/\text{unemployed}_t)$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Universe} The 49 French industries are mapped to the 13 JOLTS/CES industry groups
and value-weighted within each group (\cref{app:mapping}). The mapping is coarse: labor data
describe all establishments in an industry, while returns describe listed firms only. We return
to this in \cref{sec:robust}.

\paragraph{Point-in-time data} Labor data are revised for months after first release. Using
today's revised values would leak information. We rebuilt every series as it stood on each
decision date from ALFRED vintages (78 series), and kept three alternatives---first release,
fully revised, and revised with fixed publication lags---to measure how much revisions matter
(\cref{app:results}).

\paragraph{Sample} Research (signal design and model selection): May 2004--Sep 2014, 125
months. Test, opened once: Nov 2014--Aug 2026, 142 months. \todo{Figure: timeline of $T_t$ with

\subsection{Implementation}

\paragraph{Signals} For a labor series $x_{g,t}$ in industry group $g$, let
$\bar x_{g,t}=\tfrac13(x_{g,t}+x_{g,t-1}+x_{g,t-2})$. With publication lag $\ell$ (2 months for
JOLTS, 1 for CES),
\begin{equation}
F_{g,t}=\bar x_{g,t-\ell}-\bar x_{g,t-\ell-12}
\qquad\text{(log differences for hours and earnings).}
\end{equation}
Each signal is scaled by its own expanding-window volatility (at least 24 months), then
z-scored across the 13 groups and winsorised at $\pm3$, giving $z^{(k)}_{g,t}$.

\paragraph{Models} \Cref{tab:models} summarises the five preregistered strategies. Learned
models are ridge regressions of next-month relative return on the signals,
\begin{equation}
\hat\beta_t=\arg\min_\beta \sum_{s<t}\sum_g\big(r^{\mathrm{rel}}_{g,s+1}-z_{g,s}^{\top}\beta\big)^2+\alpha\lVert\beta\rVert^2,
\end{equation}
re-estimated each month on an expanding window, with $\alpha\in[0.01,1000]$ chosen by blocked
cross-validation inside the research period.

\paragraph{Portfolio, risk model and costs} Each month the score is converted to
dollar-neutral industry weights $\tilde w_{g,t}$ (long the highest-ranked groups, short the
lowest). Because the forecast is for one month but labor news is slow, we hold each book for
three months in overlapping tranches,
$w_{g,t}=\tfrac13\sum_{j=0}^{2}\tilde w_{g,t-j}$. The book is hedged to zero market beta (60-month
betas) and scaled to a 10\% ex-ante volatility using a 60-month covariance matrix, subject to
gross exposure $\le 2$. Costs are 10\,bp per unit of industry turnover plus 2\,bp for the
hedge; results at 0 and 25\,bp are in \cref{app:results}. \todo{verify the weight formula against
backtest() in the frozen code and write it as an equation}

\begin{keybox}{How we kept the test honest}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Every input as it was known on the decision date (ALFRED vintages).
  \item Signals, models, primary strategy and pass/fail gates fixed and hashed before any test

  \item Every result reported whatever its sign; a circular-shift placebo with full refits,

  \item Anything decided after seeing test results is labelled \tagp; the live out-of-sample

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Robustness and limits: why it failed}\label{sec:robust}

\paragraph{Wrong signs, missing signal} Single-signal ICs (\cref{tab:hyp}) show layoffs and
hours enter A0 with the wrong sign, and openings carry no information. Wage growth, which the
original blueprint included and the build dropped, is the only signal with a consistent IC:
$+0.039$ in research and $+0.041$ in test. % src: diagnostics.md §3
Both agent runs independently rediscovered it from the anonymised variable V05 without being
told what it was.

\paragraph{Wrong horizon} At one month no hiring signal predicts returns; at twelve months
hires ($-0.062$, $t=-2.1$) and quits ($-0.065$, $t=-1.7$) carry the negative sign the
investment channel predicts \citep{BeloLinBazdresch2014}. % src: diagnostics.md §4
We traded the demand channel at a horizon where only the cost channel shows up.
\todo{IC-by-horizon heatmap (fig\_horizon.pdf)}

\paragraph{An untestable moderator} Tightness terciles were set on an expanding window. Because
the research period never saw a tight market, 132 of 141 test months fell into the ``high''
tercile, so the tightness model was effectively a single regime. % src: diagnostics.md §5
A balanced alternative---$T$ rising versus falling over 12 months---splits the test 88/53.
\todo{IC in T-rising vs T-falling months (fig\_tightness.pdf), labelled POST-HOC}

\paragraph{Fragile agent features} The selected A3 features were gated on the ``low tightness''
regime and were active in only 3 of 142 test months. Every feature that passed research failed
the test (4/4 Sonnet, 6/6 Opus). Regime conditioning produced the highest research $t$-statistics
in the whole search ($3.56$) and nothing out of sample. % src: diagnostics.md §6 ; FINDINGS §4.4
\todo{scatter research IC vs test IC, selected and regime-gated features marked (fig\_agents\_scatter.pdf)}

\paragraph{Concentrated losses} Health care alone cost the primary 0.26 of cumulative return;
construction and finance followed. \todo{P\&L by industry bar chart (fig\_industry\_pnl.pdf)}
% src: diagnostics.md §2

\paragraph{Over-shrinkage and a binding risk cap} Cross-validation chose the largest ridge
penalty on the grid ($\alpha=1000$) for every model, and the gross-exposure cap, not the
volatility target, bound in 80--93\% of months. % src: diagnostics.md §1, §7

\paragraph{Limits} 142 test months can only detect a Sharpe of about 0.6 at $t\approx2$; a null
does not rule out a smaller effect. The 49-to-13 industry mapping dilutes the labor signal.

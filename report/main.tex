\section{Executive summary}
% <= 225 words. Order: question, design, result, why, what survives, forward test, Claude.
Can public labor-market flows predict which of 13 U.S.\ industry groups outperform? We rank the groups each month on the year-over-year change in openings, hires, quits, layoffs, hours and wages, as known at the time, and hold a market-neutral book long the top three and short the bottom three. The design was preregistered, frozen and opened once on 142 untouched months (November 2014 to August 2026). The primary strategy lost 4.6\% a year after costs (Sharpe $-0.58$); its forecasts were uncorrelated with returns and indistinguishable from a placebo. % src: claims C01, C02, C03, C05
We conclude: \emph{Do not implement}. It failed because the preregistered signs of layoffs and hours were wrong, the one stable one-month signal (wage growth) was dropped during the build, and the labor market never tightened in the research years, so tightness went untested. % src: claims D03, D01, D06
The data are consistent with a slower, opposite effect, as investment-based asset pricing predicts: industries that hire and lose workers fastest earn lower returns over the following 9 to 24 months. A 12-month book on that sign is post hoc, lost money in the first test half and does not survive deflation, % src: claims R4a, R12
though positive in every window (test Sharpe $+0.29$); % src: claim V_V2d_SR
it runs as a paper book in a forward test frozen on 3 October 2026. % src: claim F05
Claude was a useful auditor and idea generator when a human checked every claim and it could not change the rules after seeing results. % src: docs/team_inputs/al_claude_section.md (bottom line)

\section{What did we try? Labor flows as industry news}

\subsection{Idea: demand news, cost news and tightness}

Our thesis is that the labor market reports an industry's prospects before its earnings do. The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) publishes job openings, hires, quits and layoffs by industry every month in the Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey (JOLTS), and hours and earnings in the Current Employment Statistics (CES). The series are free and timely, while published industry-rotation strategies rely on price momentum \citep{MoskowitzGrinblatt1999,ZarattiniAntonacci2024}. We state three hypotheses (\cref{fig:thesis}).

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{H1, demand channel} (preregistered and tested). Rising openings, hires and hours signal growing demand for an industry's output before it reaches reported sales, so the industry outperforms over the next one to three months \citep{HongTorousValkanov2007}.
  \item \emph{H2, cost and investment channel} (tested post hoc). Firms that hire heavily convert growth options into assets in place and pay adjustment costs, so their expected returns fall over the following 6 to 24 months \citep{BeloLinBazdresch2014}; wage pressure squeezes margins, and labor-market frictions make labor-intensive firms riskier \citep{KuehnSimutinWang2017,Donangelo2019}.
  \item \emph{H3, tightness moderator.} When vacancies outnumber the unemployed, hiring is expensive and H2 should dominate H1. We measure tightness by $T_t=\log(\text{job openings}_t/\text{unemployed}_t)$; $T>0$ means more vacancies than unemployed ($V/U>1$).
\end{itemize}

The preregistered strategy traded H1 at a one-month horizon. H2's horizon range was written down on 3 October 2026, after the test had been opened and after the horizon profile in \cref{fig:horizon} had been seen; it carries the post-hoc label wherever it appears.

\paragraph{Universe, a design constraint} The 49 French industries are mapped to the 13 JOLTS/CES industry groups and value-weighted within each group (\cref{app:mapping}). With 13 groups the achievable information ratio is small: by the fundamental law of active management (\cref{sec:robust}) it is about $\mathrm{IC}\times\sqrt{\mathrm{BR}}\times\mathrm{TC}$, where BR is breadth (independent bets a year) and TC the transfer coefficient (the correlation between the signal and the weights held); a monthly book on 13 groups has BR of at most $13\times12=156$ and a 12-month book 13, so a monthly IC of 0.05 caps the ratio near 0.6 and a 12-month IC of 0.10 near 0.35. % src: claim R11a (breadth per year)
The mapping is also coarse: labor data describe every establishment in an industry, returns describe listed firms, and durable manufacturing is 60\% semiconductors by market capitalisation. % src: claim S03

\paragraph{Point-in-time data} Labor data are revised after first release, so today's values leak information. The frozen study rebuilt every series as it stood on each decision date from ALFRED, the St.\ Louis Fed archive of data vintages (78 series), % src: 230ga-follow-the-workers/outputs/follow_the_workers/report/report.md section 2b (78 core first-vintage dates)
and kept three alternatives: first release, fully revised, and revised with fixed publication lags. The primary result barely depends on the choice: A3's test Sharpe is $-0.58$ as known, $-0.52$ on first releases, $-0.56$ revised and $-0.53$ with fixed lags. % src: claim C20
Our post-hoc work (\cref{sec:survives}) uses revised data with fixed lags, which reproduces the frozen fixed-lag signal with a correlation of 0.998 across all group-months; % src: claim S01
as-known data pick the same top-three groups in only 42\% of months, so post-hoc holdings, though not the conclusions, would have differed in real time. % src: claim S02
One exception, found in the final code review: the federal shutdown of October--November 2025 delayed BLS releases, so two post-hoc earning months (November and December 2025) use labor data published after the decision date; the confirmatory as-known data are unaffected, and the v2/v3 numbers were left as frozen (\path{docs/process/OPEN_ISSUES.md}). % src: analysis/output/data_vintage.md (Known exception)

\subsection{Implementation: signals, models, portfolio and AI agents}

\paragraph{Signals} For a labor series $x_{g,t}$ in group $g$, let $\bar x_{g,t}=\tfrac13(x_{g,t}+x_{g,t-1}+x_{g,t-2})$. With publication lag $\ell$ (2 months for JOLTS, 1 for CES),
\begin{equation}
F_{g,t}=\bar x_{g,t-\ell}-\bar x_{g,t-\ell-12}\qquad\text{(log differences for hours and earnings).}
\end{equation}
Each signal is divided by its own expanding-window standard deviation (at least 24 months), z-scored across the 13 groups and winsorised at $\pm3$, giving $z^{(k)}_{g,t}$. Year-over-year differences remove seasonality, so the not-seasonally-adjusted series that exist for every industry can be used.

\paragraph{Models} \Cref{tab:models} lists the five preregistered strategies, all of them H1 books. The learned ones are ridge regressions of next-month relative return on the signals,
\begin{equation}
\hat\beta_t=\arg\min_\beta \sum_{s<t}\sum_g\big(r^{\mathrm{rel}}_{g,s+1}-z_{g,s}^{\top}\beta\big)^2+\alpha\lVert\beta\rVert^2,
\end{equation}
re-estimated each month on an expanding window, with $\alpha$ chosen once by cross-validation on contiguous time blocks with a one-month purge, inside the research period, over a grid from 0.01 to 1000. Cross-validation chose 1000, the strongest shrinkage the grid allowed, for every model. % src: claim C18

\begin{keybox}[How we kept the test honest]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Every input as it was known on the decision date (ALFRED vintages); the primary result is unchanged under four data vintages. % src: claim C20
  \item Signals, models, the primary strategy and five pass/fail gates (G1 timing, G2 prediction, G3 incremental alpha, G4 robustness, G5 implementability) were fixed and hashed before any test data were touched; the test period was opened exactly once.

\paragraph{AI-assisted signal search}\label{sec:ai} We asked large language model (LLM) agents to propose new labor features from six anonymised variables using a restricted formula grammar. Two arms of three agents each, \emph{independent} versus \emph{islands} that exchange short migration reports, ran three repetitions, for 108 proposals per study (\cref{fig:agents}). % src: claims A01, A02 (108 proposals); d10_agent_design.csv (arms, agents, repetitions)
Selection was mechanical (research $t\ge1.5$, same sign in both halves, low correlation, at most three per arm). An LLM judge, audited by a human, scored whether ideas spread between agents. The agents were Claude Sonnet~5.5 in the confirmatory study and Claude Opus~5.5 in an exploratory follow-up run after the test had been seen (\cref{app:opus}).

\section{What did we learn? Slow cost news, not fast demand news}

\begin{keybox}[Answer to the thesis]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{H1, demand channel: rejected} \tagc. The primary H1 book lost 4.6\% a year (Sharpe $-0.58$), its IC was $-0.021$ ($t=-1.08$), the placebo $p$ was 0.72 and four of five gates failed. % src: claims C01, C02, C03, C05, C07
  \item \emph{H2, cost channel: consistent with the data, not established} \tagp. The hiring score has a 12-month IC of $+0.096$ ($t=2.43$) and a 12-month book is positive in every window ($+0.29$ test), but it fails the pre-declared reading rule: it lost money in the first half of the test and its deflated Sharpe is 0.05 after 132 looks. A forward test is live. % src: claims V02, V_V2d_SR, R4a, R12
  \item \emph{H3, tightness: untestable as designed.} $V/U$ never exceeded 1 in the research sample, so 133 of 142 test months fell in one tightness bin. % src: claim D06
\end{itemize}
\end{keybox}

\subsection{Style and risk exposures: partly a quality and growth tilt}
The H1 books are quality and growth tilts rather than bets on labor news. Regressed on the Fama--French five factors, momentum and an industry-momentum factor (\cref{fig:loadings}, appendix), the primary loads on profitability (RMW $+0.19$, $t=2.2$) and against value (HML $-0.15$, $t=-2.0$), with an alpha of $-0.38\%$ a month ($t=-1.86$). % src: claims C13, C04, C04b
The tilt is plausible: industries adding workers and hours are the profitable, growing ones, so a long-hiring book buys profitability and sells value. The post-hoc wage-growth book tilts the same way but harder (HML $-0.43$, $t=-4.4$; momentum $+0.22$, $t=3.3$), % src: claim V11
while the H2 book loads the other way, against profitability (RMW $-0.36$, $t=-4.45$). % src: claim R6b

\subsection{Performance over time: every preregistered book lost}
The H1 primary lost money in every window except the last 18 months, and that bright spot ($+0.85$) rests on an IC $t$ of 0.44 (\cref{fig:cumulative}; \cref{tab:windows} in \cref{app:results} has every window). % src: claim C10; results.json windows.A3.last_18.IC.t

while the post-hoc H2 book is positive in every course window, although its two test halves split $-0.21$ and $+0.64$ (\cref{tab:v3}). % src: claims V_V2d_SR, R4a

\subsection{Robustness and limits: why H1 failed, what survives}\label{sec:robust}

\paragraph{What 13 industries can deliver} In the test the fundamental law implies IRs of $-0.21$ for A3 (IC $-0.021$, TC 0.80), $+0.56$ for V2a and $+0.17$ for the H2 book (12-month IC $+0.096$, TC 0.48) against realised $-0.58$, $+0.16$ and $+0.29$; % src: claim R11a
the preregistered overlay (hedge, volatility target, binding cap, 0.9 turnover a month) cost the monthly books most of what their IC allowed. % src: claim C21

\paragraph{Why H1 failed: four causes} Four post-hoc explanations (figures in \cref{app:results}).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Signs not supported by the data.} Layoffs carry a positive IC in both periods and hours a negative one, the opposite of the signs A0 imposed (\cref{tab:hyp}); wage growth, dropped during the build, is the only signal whose IC exceeds 1.5 standard errors in both periods ($+0.029$ research, $+0.049$ test). % src: claims D03, D01; d1_signal_ic.csv (research_t, test_t)
  \item \emph{Wrong horizon.} At one month no hiring signal predicts returns; at twelve months hires ($-0.062$, $t=-2.1$) and quits ($-0.065$, $t=-1.7$) carry the H2 sign. % src: claim D04
  \item \emph{An untestable moderator (H3).} Tightness terciles were set on an expanding window, so 133 of 142 test months fell in the ``high'' tercile; a split on whether $T$ rose or fell over 12 months (88/54 test months) would have been testable. % src: claims D06, D07
  \item \emph{Fragile agent features.} The two features selected for the primary were gated to the ``low tightness'' regime and active in 3 of 142 test months; % src: claim D12
  every selected feature, in both studies, failed the frozen test criterion. % src: claim C19
\end{enumerate}
Losses concentrated in health care (26 points of cumulative return), construction (19) and finance (16), against 13 points of trading costs. % src: claim D09

\paragraph{What survives: the 12-month cost channel}\label{sec:survives} None of the four post-hoc variants meets the pre-declared reading rule (positive Sharpe at 25\,bp, IC $t\ge2$, alpha $t\ge2$, positive IC in both test halves, placebo $p<0.05$, DSR above 0.95). We froze them in a hashed file (\path{analysis/v2_spec.md}) after the test was opened, ran each once and report all four (\cref{tab:v2}, \cref{fig:v2_windows}). % src: claim V07
The declared headline, a wage-growth rank (V2a), has the right IC ($+0.049$, $t=2.04$, placebo $p=0.02$) but no alpha ($t=0.29$) and a DSR of 0.02; % src: claim V01
a composite of all six signals with signs learned on research data (V2b) is negative, and the average rank of wage growth and 12-month industry momentum (V2c) reaches $+0.35$ because momentum supplies the return. % src: claims V_V2b_SR, V_V2c_SR, V05

V2d is the H2 book: $-\tfrac12(z(\text{F2})+z(\text{F3}))$, short the industries that hire and lose workers fastest, held twelve months. Its score has no one-month IC ($-0.020$, $t=-0.87$) and a positive IC from 9 to 24 months, peaking at 12 ($+0.096$, $t=2.43$; \cref{fig:horizon_profile}); its alpha is $+0.26\%$ a month ($t=1.48$ test, $1.92$ full sample, 12 Newey--West lags; \cref{tab:v2} uses 6), and it fails the rule. % src: claims R2a, R6a, R6a2
Where V2d came from matters. Its sign is the one the investment channel predicts \citep{BeloLinBazdresch2014}; its 12-month horizon was chosen from a horizon profile that included the test period (\cref{fig:horizon}), so the horizon is fitted on the test. On the research years alone the 12-month IC is $+0.047$ ($t=0.90$) with a horizon-matched placebo $p$ of 0.26: % src: claim V13
the research sample alone would not have selected it.

\section{Opus follow-up study (exploratory)}\label{app:opus}
After the test had been opened, the agent experiment was rerun with Claude Opus~5.5 at extra-high effort, with fresh agents and the same frozen evaluator, selection rule and portfolio. Because the test period was already known to the team, the run is exploratory. Its research-selected primary, A4, earned a test Sharpe of $+0.01$ (research $-0.40$) with an IC of $-0.002$ ($t=-0.10$), an alpha $t$ of 0.05, a placebo $p$ of 0.56 and a Sharpe of $-0.19$ at 25\,bp; it failed gates G2 to G5 and had a Sharpe of $-1.41$ over the last 18 months. % src: claims X01, X02

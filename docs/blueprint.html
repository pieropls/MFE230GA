230GA – Project

Follow the Workers: labor-conditioned industry rotation, tested with blinded research agents

Team Piero Pelosi · Alex Roesler · Romain Almeida · Elouan Bahri · Al Yazid Bensaid

We predictWhich of 13 US industry groups will beat the others next month

A cross-sectional ranking of 1-month relative returns, not the direction of the market.

WithMonthly BLS labor data per industry, exactly as published

JOLTS openings, hires, quits, layoffs; CES weekly hours and hourly earnings; read as known on each decision date (ALFRED vintages). National tightness T = log(openings ÷ unemployed) changes how the signals are read.

For how longPredict 1 month, hold 3 months

Signals update monthly. Each position is held 3 months (three overlapping tranches). 6 months is reported, never used to decide: too few independent observations.

How we tradeLong the top 3, short the bottom 3

Dollar-neutral, market-beta hedged, 10% volatility target, gross cap 2×, cost charged on every trade (10 bp base).

How we testResearch on 2004–2014, one sealed run on 2014–2026

All choices frozen with a hash, then the sealed period (as-known data, about Nov 2014 → Aug 2026) runs once. A five-part gate decides. “Do not implement” is the expected outcome, and the report is built so it is still informative.

The AI experimentBlinded ChatGPT agents propose features

3 “islands” that share short findings vs 3 independent agents, 18 candidates per run, 3 seeds per arm; only seed 1 can feed the strategy. Company-level postings (LinkUp) and layoff notices (WARN) join only if the day-1 audit passes.

Q1Does public labor information predict industry returns?
Q2Does company-level recruiting and layoff data add to it?
Q3Do agents that share findings search better than agents that do not?Answered on the research period, 18 candidates per arm and seed. The sealed A4 vs A3 comparison is one draw and is reported descriptively, not as a test.

Team decisions, made before day 1

  • Company data drop without discussion if day 1 runs over. The gate is time-boxed at 3 hours; Q2 is secondary. The evaluator and as-of panels are what everything else waits on.
  • Any slack goes to cross-sectional width, not A2. With shared and approximate CES series, effective width is about 8–9 groups, not 13. NAICS-based CRSP baskets would raise the attainable Sharpe for a given IC more than any agent refinement. They are the named follow-up, not part of this run.

Why 1-month prediction with 3-month holding

Horizon and overfitting

You were right to worry. With S* ≈ October 2014, the sealed test has 142 monthly returns. At a 6-month horizon that is only 23 non-overlapping observations, and all 13 groups move with the same few labor cycles. So the model is trained and tested on 1-month returns, positions are held 3 months to match your trading horizon, and 6 months is described but never decides anything.

Ten controls against overfitting

  1. Pre-registration. Every constant lives in params.py, hashed before any return is loaded.
  2. Sealed test in code. Test-period returns load only inside the final function, after the freeze hash matches.
  3. Few parameters. The fixed-rule benchmark has none; the ridge has one penalty, chosen once on research data.
  4. Transformations fixed in advance. Year-over-year changes of 3-month averages; no lookback search.
  5. Bounded search. 36 agent candidates in total, 14-operation grammar, at most 3 nested; invalid tries count. Replication seeds are run for process measurement only and are excluded from the candidate library by pre-registration.
  6. Everything logged, including failures; the trial count feeds the deflated Sharpe ratio.
  7. Library-level test. Reality check of the best candidate against all 36, not just the winner.
  8. Selection on research data only, including the choice of primary strategy, before the seal opens.
  9. Dependence-aware inference. Newey-West errors, block bootstrap, circular time-shift placebo.
  10. Honest counting. Report independent periods, not industry-months; shared macro series are not independent evidence.

STEP 2

Pipeline, fully detailed

The stages in the order they run. Each lists its rules and its output file. The evaluator (6) is built before the agents (5) run, so the agents can never shape it.

Day 1 · before any return

0 · Pre-register

→ params.py, prereg_v0.json
  • Every constant on this page goes into params.py: dates, universe, series IDs, formulas, windows, grammar, budgets, seeds, costs, comparison family, gate thresholds.
  • Six statements are written verbatim as pre-registration text: tightness is extrapolated, the sealed boundary stays at S*, the seed roles, the trial-count scope, the Holm family, and the expected outcome.
  • SHA-256 of params.py saved with a timestamp. Later changes are allowed only for reasons unrelated to performance, logged in deviations.md.
Day 1

1 · Download and audit

→ manifest.csv, checkpoints.md
  • Download every file in Step 1; validate every series ID through the FRED API; cache raw responses.
  • Vintage audit: first vintage per series. A series is admitted if its first vintage is on or before 30 Jun 2015; a measure is admitted if ≥ 11 of 13 groups have it. S* = latest first vintage among admitted series.
  • If fewer than 3 JOLTS measures pass, the primary switches to pseudo-real-time and every result says so.
  • F6 price matches: exact NAICS-scope PPIs only; F6 enters models only with ≥ 9 groups.
  • Company-data gate: LinkUp needs created history from 2008, a point-in-time company mapping, scrape-failure detection and ≥ 20 cohort companies per group-month in ≥ 10 groups. WARN needs a company mapping covering ≥ 10 groups from 2008. Otherwise skipped, with the reason. Time-boxed at 3 hours: if access is not working by then, company data and A2 drop without discussion.
  • P1 (ChatGPT): audit the manifest, crosswalk and as-of code; confirmed errors are fixed before Stage 2.
Day 1–2

2 · As-of panels

→ panel_asof.parquet + variants
  • For each decision date d, take the value with the latest realtime_start ≤ d − 1 day (fredapi all releases + merge_asof).
  • Research period (before S*): no vintages exist, so use the S* vintage with fixed lags (JOLTS ≤ d − 2, CES and CPS ≤ d − 1). No later revision leaks in.
  • Variants from the same code: ASOF (primary), FIRST (first releases, robustness), REVISED (today's values, hindsight diagnostic), FIXEDLAG (sensitivity).
  • 5 random values hand-checked against the ALFRED website; asserts that no observation is newer than allowed.
Day 2

3 · Features

→ features.parquet
  • avg3(X, m) = mean of months m, m−1, m−2. JOLTS use the latest month m_J; CES the latest m_C.
F1 openings = avg3(JOR, m_J) − avg3(JOR, m_J − 12) F2 hires = avg3(HIR, m_J) − avg3(HIR, m_J − 12) F3 quits = avg3(QUR, m_J) − avg3(QUR, m_J − 12) F4 layoffs = avg3(LDR, m_J) − avg3(LDR, m_J − 12) F5 hours = log avg3(H, m_C) − log avg3(H, m_C − 12) F6 wage–price = Δ12 log avg3(E) − Δ12 log avg3(P) (only if admitted) T = log(JTSJOL / UNEMPLOY), matched month (common to all groups) A0 fixed rule = mean(z1, z2, −z4, z5) (signs set by economics, no fitting)
  • Standardize: divide by the group's own expanding standard deviation (min 24 months), then z-score across groups, winsorize ±3.
  • Tightness is extrapolated. V/U stays below 1 (T below 0) throughout research, so any feature × T effect is estimated outside the range observed there and applied to a test period containing the 2021–23 tight market. Q1 is judged on main effects; interactions are descriptive.
  • Company level first (if admitted), over a cohort with ≥ 24 months of history: new-posting growth, breadth, concentration, WARN intensity, retrenchment = mean(WARN × falling postings), reallocation = mean(WARN × rising postings), public–private divergence (same reference month; the leading version is a separate feature).
Day 2

4 · Blinding

→ blind_map.json (local, secret)
  • Variables become V01…Vk with type metadata; groups G01…G13 in random order; time t = 1…n, no dates.
  • Agents never see names, tickers, dates, vendors, series IDs, raw values or the panel. Calls go through the API with no tools; that, not a prompt instruction, is the security boundary.
Day 2 · built before agents

6 · Fixed evaluator and portfolio

→ evaluator tested on a synthetic signal
  • Target: next-month relative return (group excess return minus the cross-sectional mean).
  • Models: A1 = ridge on z-feature main effects; A1-T adds each feature × T and is descriptive only. Monthly walk-forward refit on an expanding window; penalty chosen once by 5-fold blocked CV on research data, then frozen. Optional comparator: depth-2 LightGBM, fixed settings.
  • Portfolio: each month a new tranche goes long the top 3 and short the bottom 3, equal-weight; the book is the average of the last 3 tranches. Market beta hedged (60-month betas); 10% volatility target (Ledoit-Wolf, 60 months); gross cap 2×.
  • Costs: c · Σ|w_target − w_pretrade| with c = 10 bp (0 and 25 bp sensitivities); hedge trades 2 bp; borrow and financing reported separately.
  • Feedback returned to agents, rounded to 2 decimals: coverage, mean IC, Newey-West t, IC in low vs high T, max correlation with base features, rank persistence. Never the IC in each half: Stage 7 selects on it. Keeping the T split means agents see the thesis variable during search; the report says so.
Day 3 · research data only

5 · Agent research

→ agents/log.jsonl, candidates.csv
  • Islands: 3 ChatGPT agents (starting emphases temporal, composition, interactions), 6 submissions each; after submissions 2 and 4, each writes a ≤ 120-word report, including a failure, sent to the other two. No code shared.
  • Independent control: same 3 emphases, model, prompts and evaluator, 6 submissions each, no reports.
  • 3 seeds per arm. Seed 1 is the only strategy-producing run (36 candidates). Seeds 2–3 measure search behaviour and never enter selection or the trial count.
  • Q3 process metrics, per arm and seed: best-so-far IC by submission index, median IC of valid candidates, invalid-submission rate, diversity (1 − mean pairwise Jaccard of expression tokens). Arms compared against the spread across seeds.
  • Migration tracing: each report is split into claims; a separate ChatGPT judge tags which later submissions of the receiving islands use them (team spot-checks 20%); uptake and score effect are reported.
  • Each submission: name, falsifiable hypothesis, falsification condition, expression in a 14-operation grammar (max 3 nested, 2 variables plus T, no negative lags). Invalid ones count against the budget.
  • P2 = seed-1 first submissions; P3 = seed-1 migration reports and their tracing. The engineer (the coding model) never proposes, edits or ranks features.
Day 4 · morning

7 · Select, red-team, freeze

→ selected_features.json, FREEZE.json
  • Selection per architecture (seed 1 only): research-period t ≥ 1.5, same-sign IC in both halves, max |correlation| < 0.8; up to 3 features. None qualify → strategy equals its base.
  • Strategies (final list): A0 fixed rule · A1 public ridge (main effects) · A1-T with tightness interactions (descriptive) · A2 hybrid ridge (if admitted) · A3 base + independent features · A4 base + island features (· A5 LightGBM comparator).
  • Reality check of the best candidate against the 36 seed-1 candidates; deflated Sharpe counting seed-1 candidates plus strategies. Process seeds excluded, so the library is never read as 108.
  • Primary strategy = best research-period net Sharpe among A0–A4, recorded now.
  • P4 red team (ChatGPT) on research results; its runnable tests join the prespecified diagnostics.
  • Freeze: hash of code, params and selected features.
Day 4 · once

8 · Sealed final evaluation

→ outputs/final/*
  • Every strategy on as-known data: IC at 1, 3, 6 months (overlap-robust errors), gross and net long–short returns, Sharpe, drawdown, turnover, long-only active return and IR.
  • Tests (block bootstrap, Holm across 3): A1 vs A0 (Q1) · A2 vs A1 on the overlap sample (Q2) · A3 vs base. Descriptive, with intervals: A4 vs A3 (one draw of Q3), A1-T vs A1, V/U above vs below 1.
  • Research-vs-sealed accounting: each seed-1 candidate and strategy shown with both statistics; count of candidates that pass research and fail the test.
  • Placebo: circular time shift of the frozen features, every shift from 24 months to T − 24.
  • Attribution: FF5 + Mom + group momentum. Windows: halves, ex-2020, last 18 months, T terciles. Variants: first release, revised (the revision gap), fixed lag. Drop-one-group.
  • Implementation: cost breakeven, capacity, candidate ETF proxies (French portfolios are research series, not tradable).
Day 4–5

9 · Gate, decode, report

→ report.md, chatgpt_log.md, figures
  • Implement only if all hold for the primary strategy: G1 as-known timing with no open audit flags · G2 1-month IC t ≥ 2 · G3 alpha after attribution t ≥ 2 · G4 positive IC in both halves, placebo p < 0.05, no group above 50% of P&L · G5 net Sharpe > 0 at 25 bp. Otherwise: “Do not implement”, naming the failed criteria.
  • Expected outcome, pre-registered: “Do not implement”. The report leads with what carries the contribution anyway: the research-vs-sealed gap, the candidates that pass research and fail the test, and the comparison of the two search architectures.
  • Decode the blind map only now, for interpretation; nothing flows back into selection.
  • Report in the course template; four-field ChatGPT logs with a column left for the team's own critique.

Evaluation criteria from the brief

How it scores

40Thesis
  • One question anyone gets: is hiring demand news or cost news, and does tightness decide?
  • Three questions, each able to fail: Q1 and Q2 tested on the sealed period, Q3 on the search process
  • Mechanism kept apart from returns: labor data describe the economy; only the sealed test speaks to returns
40Execution
  • As-known data with a measured revision gap
  • Risk control: beta hedge, vol target, costs, turnover
  • Horizons 1, 3, 6 months with honest sample sizes
  • Windows: full, post-2010, last 18 months, labeled
  • Sealed test, placebo, paired tests, five-part gate, expected outcome stated in advance
20Originality
  • Industry JOLTS as an equity signal, conditioned on tightness
  • Blinded ChatGPT agents: islands vs independent, matched budgets, 3 seeds, migration traced claim by claim
  • Company-level interactions of layoffs and recruiting (if admitted)
  • ChatGPT scored, not just used (P1–P4)
